That keeps the published sites authoritative: nothing to sync, nothing to go stale. Anyone connects with a single URL and gets the same answers I would give, drawn from the same words I actually wrote."

  - decision: "Source content from what the sites already publish rather than a separate store"

    alternatives:
      - "Maintain a separate content store or CMS for the server"
      - "Hardcode the content into the server"
  - decision: "Expose a small, predictably named surface aligned to emerging standards"

    alternatives:
      - "A single catch-all entry point"
      - "Many narrow, category-specific ones"
  - decision: "Host it centrally instead of asking people to run it themselves"
    reasoning: "Consumers connect with one URL and no setup, and improvements reach everyone at once instead of waiting for each person to update."

The honest reason it exists: people kept asking AIs to act like me, and I wanted those answers to come from something real. It also gave me a firsthand feel for how these tools reach for context, which is the same question I ask about a design system: can someone find the right thing by name, or do they guess?

In practice an assistant asks for a piece of my work the way you would name it, `donnie://projects/miseenmode` or `blog://posts/truly-semantic`, and an `ask-damato` prompt lets a client ask for my answer specifically when several sources are connected. Point any compatible client at `https://mcp.damato.design/` to connect, though it is paused for now to keep hosting costs down.
